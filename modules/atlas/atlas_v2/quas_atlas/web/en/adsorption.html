Adsorption and magnetism

Why hydrogen holds on platinum and not on gold, why iron is magnetic and copper is not, and why PBE turns the insulator NiO into a metal. Three simple models: an adsorbate level on the d band of a metal, the Stoner criterion and the two atoms of Hubbard — and the DFT+U correction that follows from them.

What to understand

  • The adsorption energy: Eads = Eslab+ads − Eslab − Egas, all three calculations with the same settings; negative means adsorption pays. For atomic hydrogen the reference is half a molecule H₂.
  • Chemisorption: the adsorbate level and the levels of the metal give bonding and antibonding states. The bond is strong when the antibonding states are above the Fermi level and empty. The higher the centre of the d band, the stronger the adsorption (Hammer and Nørskov).
  • Ferromagnetism after Stoner: turning spins costs kinetic energy but gains exchange; a moment appears when I·N(EF) > 1 — in narrow bands with the Fermi level inside (Fe, Co, Ni).
  • The Hubbard model: hopping t and repulsion U on an atom. For U ≫ t the electrons localize, the exchange J ≈ 4t²/U is antiferromagnetic; a substance metallic by band theory becomes a Mott insulator.
  • PBE underestimates U (the self-interaction error), and oxides of 3d metals come out with a small gap or as metals. DFT+U adds a penalty (U/2)·n(1 − n): filled orbitals go down by U/2, empty ones up, the gap grows by U. U is a parameter; energies with different U are not compared.

The Newns–Anderson model: the level εa coupled to 80 levels of a semi-elliptic d band. ΔE = 2Σ(filled with the coupling) − 2Σ(filled without it) + 2αV²·f, where f is the filled share of the d band, α = 0.05 eV⁻¹ (the Pauli repulsion). The centres of the d bands are those of Hammer and Nørskov, the widths approximate. The sp band, the same for all the metals, is left out: the numbers show the trend, not measured energies.

E(m) = Ekin(n↑) + Ekin(n↓) − 2Ekin(n/2) − I·m²/4, m = n↑ − n↓; the d band of each spin is a semi-ellipse of five states. For small m: E ≈ (m²/4)·(1/N(EF) − I). The parameters of the metals are chosen so that the model gives their moments: Fe ≈ 2.2–2.6 μB, Ni ≈ 0.6 μB.

The Hubbard model and the DFT+U correction

Repulsion U, in units of t
Ueff for DFT+U, eV

Two atoms, two electrons: ET = 0, ES = U/2 − √(U²/4 + 4t²); J = ET − ES → 4t²/U for U ≫ t. DFT+U (Dudarev): EU = (U/2)Σni(1 − ni), Vi = U(1/2 − ni). NiO: the experiment gives a gap above 4 eV, PBE about 1 eV.

Why does hydrogen hardly hold on gold in the d-band model?

The centre of the d band of gold is −3.6 eV. Both the bonding and the antibonding states of the adsorbate with the d band lie below the Fermi level and are filled: there is no gain, the Pauli repulsion remains. For nickel (−1.3 eV) part of the antibonding states is above the Fermi level — the bond is strong.

Palladium: I ≈ 0.7 eV, N(EF) ≈ 1.14 states per eV per spin. Will it be a ferromagnet?

No: the product is ≈ 0.8, the Stoner criterion I·N(EF) > 1 is not met. Palladium is «almost» a ferromagnet: its magnetic susceptibility is strongly enhanced, and small impurities of iron make it magnetic.

Two Hubbard atoms, U = 20t. What is the exchange J = ET − ES roughly?

J

≈ 0.2t: for U ≫ t J ≈ 4t²/U = 4/20 = 0.2t (exactly 0.198t). Each electron sits on its own atom, and the singlet is below the triplet only through virtual hops.

DFT+U, U = 5 eV, an orbital half filled (n = ½). What is the penalty EU for this orbital?

EU, eV

0.63 eV: EU = (U/2)·n(1 − n) = 2.5·¼ = U/8. For n = 0 and n = 1 the penalty is zero. The potential U(½ − n) moves a filled orbital down by U/2 and an empty one up by U/2.
