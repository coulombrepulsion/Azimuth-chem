Band structure and density of states

The levels of all points k make bands. The bands and the density of states (DOS) tell whether we have a metal or an insulator, what the band gap is and of which atoms it is made (PDOS). This is what is plotted from a Quantum ESPRESSO calculation.

What to understand

  • A band structure is E(k) along a path through the special points of the zone (Γ, X, M, K…); the DOS g(E) is how many states fall into an interval of energy.
  • The Fermi level inside a band — a metal; in a gap — an insulator or a semiconductor. The gap runs from the top of the valence band to the bottom of the conduction band.
  • The PDOS splits the DOS by atoms and orbitals: the conduction band is usually on the atoms with higher orbital energies.
  • Graphene: the bands touch at the point K (the Dirac cone); different atoms on the sites (h-BN) open a gap.
  • Nearly free electrons: a weak potential opens a gap 2|V| at the zone boundary; a plane-wave basis converges as waves are added.

Check your knowledge

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

Δ = 0, β₁ = β₂, two electrons per cell (one per atom). A metal or an insulator?

A metal: the gap 2√(Δ²/4 + (β₁ − β₂)²) = 0, the lower band is full, but the upper one starts at the same energy. Unequal β₁ and β₂ open a gap — this is how polyacetylene becomes a semiconductor (the Peierls transition).

Δ = 1, β₁ = β₂ = −1. How wide is the gap?

Gap, |β|

1: at the zone boundary cos ka = −1, and E± = ±Δ/2 — the gap equals Δ.

Graphene: what is the energy of the upper band at Γ with β = −2.8 eV?

E, eV

8.4 eV = 3|β|: at Γ all three neighbours add in phase. At M the energy is ±|β| = 2.8 eV, at K zero (the Dirac cone).

Nearly free electrons, V = 0.3. What is the gap between the first and the second band at the zone boundary?

Gap

0.6 ≈ 2|V|: at the zone boundary the two waves e±iπx/a have one energy, the potential mixes them and pushes them apart by 2|V|. With one plane wave (M = 0) there is no gap.
