The Bloch theorem and k-points

In a crystal the potential repeats with the period of the lattice, and every state of an electron is labelled by a wave vector k. A calculation of a crystal is a calculation at a set of points k (a k-mesh) in a basis of plane waves. Here you see where k comes from, why metals need a dense mesh and what Ecut sets.

What to understand

  • The Bloch theorem: ψk(x + a) = eikaψk(x). The vectors k and k + 2π/a give one state, so k is taken in the first Brillouin zone (−π/a…π/a).
  • The energy of a crystal is an integral over the zone; in a calculation it is a sum over k-points. For an insulator it converges fast, for a metal slowly: the Fermi level cuts the band.
  • The symmetry of the lattice reduces a k-mesh to a few inequivalent points with weights (in the Quantum ESPRESSO output — «number of k points»).
  • A plane-wave basis: the waves with |k + G|² ≤ Ecut; their number grows as V·Ecut3/2. The density needs 4·Ecut (ultrasoft pseudopotentials 8–12).

A chain: k = 0.3 π/a and k = 2.3 π/a (choose «k + 2π/a»). Are the two states the same on the atoms?

Yes. Between the atoms the waves differ, but an electron is described by the values on the lattice, and there the factor ei2πn = 1. This is why every k is brought into the first zone.

A 4 × 4 mesh shifted by ½. How many points have to be computed?

Points

3: the points (1/8, 1/8), (3/8, 1/8), (3/8, 3/8) with weights 4, 8, 4. A mesh with the Γ point would give 6 different points.

Where does the energy per cell converge faster with the number of k-points?

In an insulator: the function under the integral is smooth, and the sum over the mesh converges exponentially. In a metal the function breaks off at the Fermi level — the error falls slowly and jumps.

Silicon, Ecut = 30 Ry. How many plane waves does an orbital have at Γ?

Waves

749: the vectors with |G|² ≤ 30 bohr⁻². 5985 and 16889 are the density vectors at 120 and 240 Ry: Quantum ESPRESSO prints them as «Smooth grid» and «Dense grid».
