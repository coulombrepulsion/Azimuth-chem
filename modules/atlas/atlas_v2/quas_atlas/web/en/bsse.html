Basis set superposition error

In a calculation on a complex, each molecule can “borrow” the basis functions of its neighbour and lower its own energy — functions that are absent when the molecule is computed alone. The difference looks like attraction, but it is an artefact of the incomplete basis: the basis set superposition error (BSSE). Here the binding energy of the water dimer is computed in eleven basis sets (MP2 in ten, CCSD(T) in nine) without a correction and with the Boys–Bernardi counterpoise correction, and compared with an estimate of the complete basis set limit.

What to understand

  • In an incomplete basis each molecule of a complex “borrows” the functions of its neighbour; this gives a spurious attraction — the basis set superposition error.
  • The counterpoise correction computes the monomers in the basis of the whole complex, with ghost atoms in place of the partner.
  • BSSE is largest in small basis sets and for correlated methods; it decreases quickly as the basis grows.
  • The spurious attraction deepens the potential well and shifts the minimum to shorter distances.
Model limits: one complex, rigid monomers
What is computed
Binding energy of the water dimer without and with the counterpoise correction: HF in eleven basis sets, MP2 in ten, CCSD(T) in nine; MP2 curves along the O···O distance.
Which approximations are made
The monomers are not relaxed; a single orientation; the complete basis set limit is estimated as HF in aug-cc-pVQZ plus the MP2 correlation extrapolated from aug-cc-pVDZ/aug-cc-pVTZ (the average of corrected and uncorrected values), plus the CCSD(T) − MP2 difference in aug-cc-pVDZ; the uncertainty of the estimate is about 0.2 kcal/mol. The 6-31G* basis here has spherical d functions (5 per atom); other Atlas pages use Cartesian ones (6). The reference −4.86 kcal/mol is for rigid monomers; the “Intermolecular interactions” page quotes another estimate, 21.0 kJ/mol (5.02 kcal/mol).
What can be concluded
How large the BSSE is in different basis sets and what the counterpoise correction gives — yes; the exact binding energy of the water dimer — no: that also needs the monomer deformation energy and a geometry optimization.

How the counterpoise correction works

Ghost atoms
The monomer energies are computed in the full basis of the dimer: the partner’s basis functions are kept in place, but its nuclei and electrons are removed. Then in ΔECP = EAB(AB) − EA(AB) − EB(AB) all three energies are computed in the same basis, and the “borrowing” of functions cancels.
BSSE vanishes in a complete basis
A monomer has nothing to borrow if its own basis is complete. So the error decreases quickly as the basis grows, and the counterpoise correction is a way to get an answer close to the limit in a smaller basis.
The correction does not always help
In cc-pVDZ and cc-pVTZ the correction “overshoots”: the binding comes out weaker than the limit, and without the correction it is stronger; the average of the two is closer to the limit. In basis sets with diffuse functions the uncorrected value is already close to the limit: the spurious attraction partly compensates the incompleteness of the basis. The reliable route is a large basis with diffuse functions and extrapolation.
What else to take into account
When the geometry is optimized, the monomers in the complex are deformed; the deformation energy is computed separately. BSSE also exists within a single molecule — between its fragments; in small basis sets it distorts, for example, the relative energies of conformers.

MP2, cc-pVDZ: how large is the BSSE in the binding energy of the water dimer?

BSSE, kcal/mol

About 3.5 kcal/mol: −7.34 without the correction versus −3.85 with it. That is more than half of the binding energy itself.

The same quantity in aug-cc-pVTZ?

BSSE, kcal/mol

About 0.5 kcal/mol (−5.03 versus −4.55): as the basis grows, the monomer needs less and less to “borrow” its neighbour’s functions.

Compare the BSSE for HF and MP2 in cc-pVDZ. Which method has the larger one, and why?

MP2: 3.5 kcal/mol versus 2.1 for HF. The correlation energy needs many functions of high angular momentum, and borrowed functions lower it more than the Hartree–Fock energy.

The cc-pVDZ curve: where is the minimum without and with the correction?

R without correction, Å

Without the correction — about 2.91 Å and −7.3 kcal/mol; with it — about 3.06 Å and −4.1 kcal/mol. The spurious attraction deepens the well and shifts it to shorter distances.

How many calculations are needed for a counterpoise-corrected binding energy, and in which basis is each one done?

Number of calculations

Three, all in the dimer basis: the dimer itself and each monomer with the partner’s ghost atoms. If the monomers in the complex are deformed, the deformation energy is computed separately as well — the monomers in their own and in the equilibrium geometry.
