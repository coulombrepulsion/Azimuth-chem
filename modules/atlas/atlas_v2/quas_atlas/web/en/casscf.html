Multiconfigurational methods: CASSCF

Hartree–Fock, MP2 and coupled clusters all start from a single determinant. When several configurations are equally important — in bond breaking, in a biradical, when twisting around a double bond — this foundation fails. The CASSCF method does full CI in a small “active space” of orbitals and optimizes the orbitals themselves at the same time. It describes static correlation, and dynamic correlation is added on top — for example by NEVPT2 perturbation theory. All curves were computed with PySCF in the cc-pVDZ basis.

What to understand

  • When several configurations are equally important (bond breaking, a biradical), single-determinant methods fail — this is static correlation.
  • CASSCF solves full CI in an active space of orbitals and optimizes the orbitals at the same time.
  • Natural orbital occupation numbers well between 0 and 2 are the sign of multiconfigurational character.
  • Dynamic correlation is added on top of CASSCF: NEVPT2, CASPT2, MRCI; the size of the active space is limited to about 18 orbitals.
Model limits: diatomic and small molecules, cc-pVDZ
What is computed
Curves for H₂ (RHF, UHF, CASSCF(2,2), full CI), twisting ethylene (RHF, CASSCF(2,2), NEVPT2), curves for N₂ (RHF, CCSD, CCSD(T), CASSCF(6,6), NEVPT2); natural orbital occupation numbers; the number of determinants for CAS(n,m).
Which approximations are made
The cc-pVDZ basis; a rigid ethylene geometry during twisting; NEVPT2 as the reference for N₂ instead of full CI; CCSD may not converge at large R.
What can be concluded
When one determinant is not enough and how the occupation numbers show it — yes; accurate bond dissociation energies — no: that needs larger basis sets and MRCI or similar methods.

H₂: static and dynamic correlation

The active space CAS(2,2) is two electrons in the two orbitals σg and σu. Restricted Hartree–Fock (RHF) keeps both electrons in one orbital and goes up when the bond breaks. Unrestricted (UHF) puts the α and β electrons on different atoms and gives the right energy, but at the cost of mixing spin states. CASSCF breaks the bond correctly and stays a singlet.

Ethylene: twisting around the double bond

When one CH₂ group is twisted, the π bond breaks: at 90° the π and π* orbitals are degenerate, and the ground state is a biradical with two equivalent configurations. The active space CAS(2,2) is the same two orbitals π and π*.

N₂: three bonds break at once

A triple bond needs the active space CAS(6,6): the σ, π, π orbitals and the antibonding σ*, π*, π*. Here are also CCSD and CCSD(T), the best single-determinant methods, and NEVPT2 — CASSCF with a perturbative correction for dynamic correlation. The energy of each method is measured from its own energy at 1.1 Å, near equilibrium.

How to choose active orbitals
The active space takes the orbitals whose occupation numbers differ noticeably from 2 and 0: the bonding and antibonding orbitals of breaking bonds, the unpaired d orbitals of a metal, a π system. Natural orbital occupation numbers are the main sign: between 0.02 and 1.98, the orbital belongs in the active space.
Static and dynamic
CASSCF describes static correlation — the degeneracy of several configurations. Dynamic correlation — how electrons avoid each other at short distances — it hardly includes. It is added on top: CASPT2, NEVPT2 (perturbation theory) or MRCI (CI from several reference configurations).
Size limit
Ordinary CASSCF is feasible up to about 18 electrons in 18 orbitals: about 2.4·10⁹ determinants. Beyond that come approximate solvers: DMRG, selected CI, RASSCF with a limited number of excitations.
Pitfalls
The result depends on the choice of the active space, and the optimal orbitals may “switch” between geometries or states. That is why curves are computed by passing the orbitals from point to point, and for several states a state average (SA-CASSCF) is used.

H₂, R = 2.0 Å: what is the occupation number of the σu orbital in CASSCF(2,2)?

Occupation of σu

About 0.45: σg holds 1.55 electrons, σu 0.45. The function is already clearly two-configurational, while RHF is 251 kJ/mol above full CI here.

At the same R = 2.0 Å: what is ⟨S²⟩ of the UHF function?

⟨S²⟩

About 0.90 instead of 0 for a singlet. The UHF energy is only 39 kJ/mol above full CI, but the function is a roughly equal mixture of singlet and triplet, so its spin properties and density are wrong.

Ethylene: what rotation barrier (at 90°) does CASSCF(2,2) give, kJ/mol?

Barrier, kJ/mol

About 312 kJ/mol; NEVPT2 gives 322, RHF 542. The experimental activation energy of cis–trans isomerization is about 272 kJ/mol; the computed barrier is higher because the geometry does not relax during twisting.

Why does CCSD(T) give an energy for N₂ at large R that is lower than at equilibrium?

Coupled clusters are built from the single RHF determinant, and the (T) correction is computed by perturbation theory. When the triple bond breaks, six orbitals are nearly half filled, the amplitudes are large, and the correction stops being small: it “collapses” the energy. The warning sign can be seen in advance — in the natural orbital occupation numbers.
