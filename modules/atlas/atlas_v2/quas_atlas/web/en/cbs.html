Basis set convergence and the complete basis set limit

The energy in a finite basis is not the final answer but a point on the way to the complete basis set (CBS) limit. Dunning’s cc-pVXZ basis sets are built so that this path is predictable: the Hartree–Fock energy converges quickly, the correlation energy slowly, roughly as X⁻³ in the cardinal number X (D = 2, T = 3, Q = 4, 5). Extrapolation is built on this law: two calculations in neighbouring basis sets give a better estimate of the limit than a calculation in the larger of them. All energies were computed with PySCF at experimental geometries, with the 1s electrons of heavy atoms frozen.

What to understand

  • The energy in a finite basis is a point on the way to the complete basis set limit; the cc-pVXZ basis sets make this path systematic.
  • The Hartree–Fock energy converges quickly, almost exponentially; the correlation energy slowly, as X⁻³.
  • A two-point X⁻³ extrapolation from TZ and QZ gives a better estimate of the limit than a QZ calculation.
  • CCSD(T) atomization energies in cc-pVDZ are too low by 6–33 kcal/mol, in cc-pVTZ by 1–11: quantitative conclusions need at least TZ, preferably extrapolation.
Model limits: Dunning basis sets, small molecules
What is computed
Hartree–Fock energies, MP2 and CCSD(T) correlation energies and atomization energies in the cc-pVDZ … cc-pV5Z series; two-point X⁻³ extrapolation.
Which approximations are made
Frozen core; experimental geometries; the Hartree–Fock energy is not extrapolated but taken in the larger basis; cc-pV5Z calculations only for H₂, H₂O, HF and N₂.
What can be concluded
How fast the different contributions converge and how much better extrapolation is than a single calculation — yes; exact limit values for comparison with experiment — no: core correlation and relativistic corrections are also needed.

Extrapolation to the complete basis set limit

If EX = ECBS + A·X⁻³, then from two basis sets X and Y = X + 1 the limit is ECBS = (X³EX − Y³EY) / (X³ − Y³). The formula is applied to the correlation energy; the Hartree–Fock energy is taken in the larger basis — it has almost converged already.

Basis pair

What it means for chemistry: atomization energy

Error of the CCSD(T) atomization energy in the cc-pVXZ basis relative to the limit extrapolated from cc-pVTZ and cc-pVQZ. In a small basis the molecule misses more correlation energy than the atoms do, so the binding comes out weaker.

Why correlation converges slowly

The electron cusp
The exact wavefunction has a kink where two electrons meet: as r12 → 0 it is linear in r12. Smooth Gaussian functions describe this kink only through a sum of ever higher angular momenta — hence the slow X⁻³ law.
Hartree–Fock converges quickly
In the Hartree–Fock method an electron moves in a mean field, and no cusp is needed; the energy approaches the limit roughly exponentially. Each next basis reduces its error several times, and in energy differences the remainder mostly cancels.
The cost of each step
The number of functions grows roughly as X³: water has 24, 58, 115 and 201 functions from DZ to 5Z. The CCSD(T) time grows as N⁷, so the step from QZ to 5Z costs tens of times more than the whole QZ calculation.
Practical rules
Energies in DZ are not suitable for quantitative conclusions. A reasonable minimum is TZ, better a TZ/QZ extrapolation. Explicitly correlated methods (F12) add a factor with r12 to the wavefunction and give QZ quality in a TZ basis.

Water, CCSD(T) correlation: how much energy does the step cc-pVTZ → cc-pVQZ add?

Change in correlation, mEₕ

About 19.9 mEₕ (from −275.0 to −295.0 mEₕ), i.e. 12.5 kcal/mol. For chemistry this is a huge amount: even QZ is still far from the limit in the correlation energy.

For water, how many times larger is the TZ → QZ step for the correlation energy than for the Hartree–Fock energy?

Times larger

About 2.6 times: the Hartree–Fock energy changes by 7.7 mEₕ, the correlation energy by 19.9 mEₕ. Hartree–Fock converges almost exponentially, correlation as X⁻³, and the gap only widens as X grows.

The correlation energy is −275.0 mEₕ in TZ and −295.0 mEₕ in QZ. Estimate the complete basis set limit with the X⁻³ formula.

ECBS, mEₕ

ECBS = (4³·EQZ − 3³·ETZ)/(4³ − 3³) = (64·(−295.0) − 27·(−275.0))/37 ≈ −309.6 mEₕ. The extrapolation adds almost 15 mEₕ more to QZ.

How far is the CCSD(T)/cc-pVDZ atomization energy of water from the reference value?

Too low by, kcal/mol

Too low by about 24 kcal/mol (about 10 %). A molecule has more electron pairs than the atoms, and its correlation converges more slowly, so a small basis systematically underestimates the binding energy.

Why is the Hartree–Fock energy not extrapolated with the X⁻³ law?

It converges much faster, almost exponentially; the X⁻³ law describes the correlation energy, which reproduces the electron–electron cusp only slowly. The HF energy is usually just taken in the largest basis.
