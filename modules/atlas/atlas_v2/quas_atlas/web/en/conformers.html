Conformers: which minima to look for

An optimisation finds the nearest minimum — the one the starting structure “rolls down” into. A flexible molecule, however, has many minima: rotation about every single bond gives its own conformers, and at room temperature they interconvert quickly. Here you can see where the minima come from, how the molecules are distributed among them and why they are searched by metadynamics rather than by a grid.

What to understand

  • The energy of rotation about a bond is periodic: ethane has three equal minima with a 12 kJ/mol barrier, butane has the anti and two gauche conformers 3–4 kJ/mol higher.
  • The fraction of a conformer at temperature T is proportional to exp(−E/RT): at 298 K a difference of 5.7 kJ/mol is a 10 : 1 ratio.
  • Measured properties (spectra, dipole moment, energy) are averages over the conformers; one minimum may not be enough.
  • Every rotatable bond multiplies the number of combinations by the number of its minima: a grid grows as kⁿ, so CREST explores the surface by metadynamics.
Model limits: one torsion, a rigid frame, energies instead of Gibbs energies
What is computed
The energy profile of rotation about one bond from three Fourier terms, its minima and barriers; Boltzmann fractions of the conformers; the number of starts and the time of a grid search.
Which approximations are made
The rest of the molecule is rigid; V₁–V₃ are model parameters (for butane fitted to the typical gauche +3.6 and barriers of 14 and 21 kJ/mol); the fractions use electronic energies, without zero-point vibrations and entropy, each minimum counted separately.
What can be concluded
Where conformers come from, how their fractions depend on the energies and the temperature and why a clever search is needed — yes; exact fractions need the Gibbs energies of the conformers from a calculation (CREST + DFT).

Butane at 298 K: what fraction of the molecules is anti?

Anti fraction

About 0.68: the two gauche conformers lie 3.6 kJ/mol higher, exp(−3.6/2.48) ≈ 0.23 each, 1/(1 + 2·0.23) ≈ 0.68. The other 32 % are gauche.

Ethane: how many minima, and how are the molecules distributed?

Three equal staggered minima 120° apart: 1/3 each at any temperature. The barrier V₃ = 12 kJ/mol is the eclipsed form.

10 rotatable bonds with 3 minima each: how many starting structures does a full grid need?

Starts

3¹⁰ = 59 049. At a minute per optimisation that is 41 days, and 20 bonds would give 3.5·10⁹ structures — which is why a grid is not used.

What energy window does CREST (CREGEN) keep by default?

6 kcal/mol (25 kJ/mol) above the lowest conformer; 0.05 kcal/mol is the energy threshold below which two conformers count as the same (together with an RMSD of 0.125 Å).
