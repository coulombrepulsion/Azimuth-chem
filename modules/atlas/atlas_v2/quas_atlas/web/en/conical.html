Conical intersections and biradicals

Where two electronic states come together, the Born–Oppenheimer approximation stops working: the molecule passes from one surface to another within tens of femtoseconds. And in a biradical two electrons are almost unpaired, and one determinant cannot even say whether the singlet or the triplet lies lower.

What to understand

  • Two states: E± = H̄ ± √(Δ² + H₁₂²). They are degenerate only if Δ = 0 and H₁₂ = 0 — two independent conditions. A diatomic has one coordinate: terms of the same symmetry do not cross, of different symmetry they do (H₁₂ ≡ 0).
  • A polyatomic has 3N − 6 coordinates, and two conditions single out a seam of dimension 3N − 8 (Wigner and von Neumann, 1929). Near a point of the seam E± ≈ Ē ± √(g²x² + h²y²) — a double cone in the branching plane (g the gradient difference, h the coupling vector).
  • A conical intersection is the funnel of photochemistry: internal conversion within tens to hundreds of femtoseconds, faster than fluorescence (retinal — 200 fs). One looks for the point of the seam with the lowest energy (MECI) with state-averaged CASSCF. In TD-DFT the S₀–S₁ coupling is zero: a seam of the wrong dimension; if S₁ and S₀ are closer than 0.5 eV, do not trust TD-DFT.
  • The two-centre model: the triplet −K, the covalent singlet +K, coupled by the hopping 2t to the ionic one at U. ΔE(T − S) ≈ 4t²/U − 2K: hopping pays only for electrons of opposite spins. Benzynes: o- 157, m- 88, p- 16 kJ/mol — t falls as the centres move apart.
  • Broken-symmetry DFT: the solution is a mixture of the singlet and the triplet, ⟨S²⟩_BS = 2(1 − w). The Yamaguchi formula ΔE = 2(E_T − E_BS)/(⟨S²⟩_T − ⟨S²⟩_BS); at ⟨S²⟩_BS = 1 the Ziegler sum rule E_S = 2E_BS − E_T. Restricted DFT gives the wrong sign for p-benzyne.

In a diatomic, two terms of the same symmetry come close as the bond stretches. What happens to them?

Degeneracy needs two conditions (Δ = 0 and H₁₂ = 0), and there is one coordinate. Terms of different symmetry do cross: their H₁₂ ≡ 0.

What is the dimension of the seam of a conical intersection in ethylene C₂H₄?

10: 3·6 − 6 = 12 internal coordinates, two conditions remove two. In TD-DFT the S₁/S₀ seam comes out 11-dimensional — wrong.

In the two-centre model t = 0.5 eV, U = 4 eV, K = 0. What is the splitting ΔE(T − S) by perturbation theory?

eV

4t²/U = 4·0.25/4 = 0.25 eV ≈ 24 kJ/mol; the exact 2×2 gives 0.24 eV. The singlet is lower: hopping pays only for electrons of opposite spins.

The BS solution of p-benzyne lies 10.3 kJ/mol below the triplet, ⟨S²⟩_BS = 1. What splitting does the Yamaguchi formula give?

kJ/mol

2·10.3/(2 − 1) ≈ 21 kJ/mol (the experiment 16). At ⟨S²⟩_BS = 1 this is the Ziegler sum rule: E_S = 2E_BS − E_T.
