Defects and surfaces of crystals

A real crystal is not perfect: it has vacancies, impurities and, of course, a surface. The programs compute only periodic systems, so a defect is put into a supercell and a surface into a slab with vacuum. Here you see what a defect costs, how its charge depends on the Fermi level and which traps wait in a calculation of a surface.

What to understand

  • The formation energy of a vacancy: Ef = EN−1 − (N − 1)/N · EN — the cell with the vacancy is compared with as many atoms of the perfect crystal. From the bonds: Ef ≈ 2ε, the bond energy per atom.
  • The equilibrium share of vacancies c = e^(−Ef/kT). An error of 0.3 eV in Ef is a factor of about eight at the melting point of silicon: a calculation tells which defect prevails, not how many there are.
  • A charged defect exchanges electrons with the Fermi level: Ef(q; EF) = Ef(q; 0) + q·EF. Where the lines of two charges cross is the transition level ε(q/q′) — a difference of total energies, not an orbital energy.
  • A charged supercell interacts with its copies and the background: the correction ≈ q²α/(2εL) falls as 1/L, slowly. Hundreds of atoms are needed, a hybrid functional for the gap and several starting structures.
  • A surface is a slab of N layers and vacuum: Eslab(N) = N·Ebulk + 2Aγ. The bulk energy is taken from the slope of this line: a bulk energy «from elsewhere» with an error δ gives an error of γ of Nδ/(2A), growing with the thickness.

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

Silicon, Ef = 3.6 eV, at the melting point 1687 K. What share of the sites is empty?

c

e−24.8 ≈ 2·10−11: Ef/kT = 3.6/(8.617·10−5·1687) = 24.8. One vacancy per 50 billion atoms — about 1012 per cubic centimetre.

Ef(+; 0) = 2.9, Ef(0) = 3.4, Ef(−; 0) = 4.3 eV. Where is the level ε(0/−)?

ε(0/−), eV

0.9 eV: ε(0/−) = [Ef(0) − Ef(−; 0)]/(−1 − 0) = 4.3 − 3.4. The level ε(+/0) = 3.4 − 2.9 = 0.5 eV: below it the defect is a donor, above 0.9 eV an acceptor.

Silicon (ε = 11.7), charge +1, a supercell of 216 atoms. What is the Makov–Payne correction roughly?

ΔE, eV

0.11 eV: L = 3·5.43 = 16.3 Å, ΔE = 2.84·14.4/(2·11.7·16.3). 0.16 eV is for 64 atoms; the correction falls as 1/L, slowly. 1.5 eV is what an oxide with ε = 5 and charge 2 gives.

The bulk energy was taken from a separate calculation with an error δ per atom. How does the error of the surface energy behave as the slab gets thicker?

It grows: Δγ = Nδ/(2A) — the error δ is multiplied by the number of atoms in the slab. This is why the bulk energy is taken from the slope of the line Eslab(N), computed with the same settings as the slabs.
