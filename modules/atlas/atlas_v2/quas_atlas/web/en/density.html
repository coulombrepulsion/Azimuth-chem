Electron density

The density ρ(r) = Σ Pμνχμ(r)χν(r), unlike orbitals and atomic charges, is an observable: it is measured by X-ray diffraction. The maps are built from a converged RHF calculation in the STO-3G basis set (the same H₂ and HeH⁺ as in the SCF tab), in a plane containing the bond axis. Hover over or tap the map to see the values at a point.

What to understand

  • The electron density ρ(r) is observable: it can be measured (X-ray structure analysis) and calculated.
  • The deformation density Δρ shows how electrons redistribute when a bond forms.
  • In Bader theory the bond type is judged at the critical point: the sign of ∇²ρ tells whether density is concentrated between the nuclei.
  • The electrostatic potential shows where a charged reagent will approach.

Check your knowledge

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

H₂ at equilibrium R: what is the sign of ∇²ρ at the bond critical point, and what does it mean?

∇²ρ ≈ −0.84 < 0 (ρ = 0.256): density is concentrated between the nuclei — a covalent bond in the Bader sense. A positive Laplacian is typical of closed-shell interactions.

Stretch H₂. What happens to ∇²ρ at the bond critical point?

It becomes positive (from about 3.4 bohr): density between the atoms is no longer concentrated, as in a closed-shell interaction — the atoms are hardly bonded.

Compress H₂. Why does the bond critical point disappear under strong compression?

Below ≈ 1.0 bohr the density maxima at the nuclei merge, and ρ has no minimum along the axis. Partly this is a property of the Gaussian basis: it has no cusp at the nuclei.

Deformation density of H₂: where were electrons gained and where lost? Why does the density increase at the nuclei themselves?

Gained on the bond and near the nuclei, lost outside the molecule. At the nuclei, because the molecular function is more contracted than the atomic one (ζ = 1.24 versus 1). The integral of Δρ is zero.

HeH⁺ at equilibrium R: why is there no bond critical point in a minimal basis?

H carries too few electrons, and in a Gaussian basis it has no density maximum of its own — the point appears only from about 1.74 bohr. The exact density has a maximum (cusp) at every nucleus, so this is a limitation of the basis, not a property of the molecule.

Electrostatic potential of H₂: where is it negative?

Only far out in the equatorial plane, beyond about 2.3 bohr from the axis (about −0.003 a.u.) — a sign of the quadrupole moment. Along the axis the potential is positive. For the cation HeH⁺ φ > 0 everywhere.
