Properties as derivatives of the energy

The dipole moment, the polarizability, the forces on the nuclei, the frequencies are derivatives of the energy with respect to a field or the coordinates. When a program cannot compute them analytically, they come from finite differences — and the subtleties begin: which step to take, how accurate the energy must be, why the polarizability needs diffuse functions.

What to understand

  • A property is a coefficient of the series of the energy in a perturbation: E(F) = E₀ − μF − ½αF² − …, μ = −∂E/∂F, α = −∂²E/∂F². The Hellmann–Feynman theorem: dE/dλ = ⟨Ψ|∂Ĥ/∂λ|Ψ⟩ for an exact or a fully variational function (HF, DFT, CASSCF), but not for MP2 and CC.
  • Finite differences: forward — error ∝ h, central — ∝ h², second derivative from three points — ∝ h². The noise of the energy δE gives an error δE/h (first derivative) and δE/h² (second): too small a step is as bad as too large.
  • The optimal step of the central difference is h ≈ (3δE/|f‴|)^⅓: for δE = 10⁻⁶ hartree about 0.01 angstrom. A numerical derivative costs 2 calculations per coordinate — 6N for a gradient, which is why optimizations run on analytic forces.
  • The finite-field method: α from the energies needs the SCF converged to 10⁻⁹–10⁻¹⁰ (the noise is divided by F²); from the dipoles in the field the noise is divided by F. The dipole of an ion depends on the origin: Δμ = Q·d, 4.8 D per angstrom.
  • The polarizability = 2Σ|⟨n|μ̂|0⟩|²/(E_n − E₀). The field pulls the density outward: without diffuse functions the polarizability of water is almost halved (def2-SVP 5.3, def2-SVPD 9.8 against 9.8 a.u. measured).

Finite differences: which step to take

The energy is known to 10−n hartree, n

The example of lecture 1: E(R) = De(1 − e−a(R−Re))², De = 0.17, a = 1.0, Re = 1.4 (a.u.); the derivative at R = 1.6 bohr, E′ = 0.050460. The energies are rounded to 10−n, as if the SCF converged to the n-th digit. Forward: [E(R+h) − E(R)]/h; central: [E(R+h) − E(R−h)]/(2h); second: [E(R+h) + E(R−h) − 2E(R)]/h².

Check your knowledge

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

The energies are known to 10⁻⁶ hartree (the example of lecture 1). With which step does the central difference give the best derivative?

h, bohr

0.01 bohr: an error of −9.6·10⁻⁶. At 0.1 the error of the formula (h²) gets in the way, at 10⁻⁴ and below the rounding (δE/h). The formula hopt = (3δE/|E‴|)⅓ gives 0.017.

The step of the central difference was made 10 times smaller (exact energies). By how much does the error fall?

100 times: the error of the central difference is ∝ h²·f‴/6. The forward difference has an error ∝ h and would fall 10 times.

The polarizability from the energies in a field F = 10⁻⁴ a.u., the SCF converged to 10⁻⁸. What is the error of α?

δα, a.u.

≈ 2 a.u.: δα ~ 2δE/F² = 2·10⁻⁸/10⁻⁸. At F = 10⁻³ it is 0.02, at 2·10⁻³ 0.005. Hence the choice F ≈ 10⁻³ and a tight SCF convergence.

What corrects the polarizability of water in B3LYP/def2-SVP (5.3 a.u. against 9.8 measured) the most?

A diffuse shell: def2-SVPD gives 9.8. def2-TZVP without diffuse functions gives only 6.8: a larger ζ adds tight functions at the nuclei, while the field pulls the density outward.
