Density functional theory

In the Kohn–Sham method the energy is E = Ts + Vne + J[ρ] + Exc[ρ], where J is the classical repulsion of the cloud with itself, and Exc must correct everything else. The exact Exc is unknown. Here are three problems where the exact answer is known; they show where approximate functionals go wrong and why hybrid functionals are needed.

What to understand

  • In DFT the energy is a functional of the density; approximations (LDA, GGA, hybrids) differ in the exchange–correlation part.
  • Self-interaction error: in LDA an electron partly repels itself.
  • The exact E(N) is piecewise linear, and its slope jump is the fundamental gap I − A = (εLUMO − εHOMO) + Δxc; for LDA and GGA E(N) is convex (delocalization error), and there is no Δxc.
  • A fraction of exact exchange reduces this error but does not remove it completely.
Model limits: model problems with a known answer
What is computed
The self-interaction error, E(N) at fractional N, the delocalisation error for H and H₂⁺.
Which approximations are made
A single function e−ζr with the optimal ζ instead of the full self-consistent solution; the LSDA and Hartree–Fock functionals and their mixture.
What can be concluded
The sign and character of the functional errors are shown correctly; the numbers depend on the simplified form of the function.

1. Hydrogen atom: self-interaction error

A single electron has no electron–electron repulsion, so one must have Ex = −J and Ec = 0. For the exact density ρ = e−2rπ: J = 516 = 0.3125. In Hartree–Fock, exchange cancels J exactly. Approximate functionals cancel it only partially; the remainder is the self-interaction error.

2. Fractional electron number: E(N) must be piecewise linear

The exact energy at fractional N (a statistical mixture of H⁺, H and H⁻) is linear between integer N, and the slope dEdN equals the energy of the highest occupied orbital. To the left of N = 1 the slope must be −I = −0.5, to the right −A = −0.0277: the jump at N = 1 is the fundamental gap. Do not confuse it with the orbital gap: in exact Kohn–Sham theory I − A = (εLUMO − εHOMO) + Δxc, where Δxc is the derivative discontinuity of the exchange-correlation energy with respect to the number of electrons (as N crosses an integer, the potential vxc itself jumps by Δxc), so even the exact KS gap is usually smaller than I − A. 3. H₂⁺: delocalization error

H₂⁺ has one electron, so Hartree–Fock is exact here (within the STO-3G basis with optimal ζ). As the nuclei are pulled apart the exact energy tends to E(H) = −0.5. A symmetric delocalized density is acceptable in itself: in the limit the descriptions “the electron on one atom” and “half an electron on each atom” must be degenerate. The error of a functional with convex E(N) is elsewhere: two halves of an electron give 2·E(½) — below the correct limit, i.e. the functional artificially favors fractional charges.

The H atom in LSDA: what should the correlation energy be, and what comes out?

A single electron has no correlation, exactly Ec = 0. LSDA gives Ec = −0.022, and exchange Ex = −0.268 instead of −0.3125: the electron “repels itself” by about 58 kJ/mol.

Why is “LDA without spin” worse than LSDA for the H atom?

For a single electron ρβ = 0, so the system is fully spin-polarized. The formula for the unpolarized gas splits the density equally between the spins and gives Ex = −0.213, a self-interaction error almost three times larger.

LSDA, a = 0: how does εHOMO of the H atom compare with the ionization potential?

εHOMO ≈ −0.27, while I = 0.5: Koopmans’ theorem does not hold for LDA. The reason is that E(N) is convex: the slope at N → 1⁻ is smaller in magnitude than the secant. So LDA and GGA orbital energies cannot be used as ionization potentials.

The exact slope jump of E(N) at N = 1 is the fundamental gap I − A. How is it related to the Kohn–Sham orbital gap?

In the exact theory I − A = (εLUMO − εHOMO) + Δxc: even the exact KS gap is smaller than I − A by the derivative discontinuity of Exc with respect to the number of electrons, Δxc (the potential vxc jumps by the same amount). For a smooth approximate functional (LDA, GGA) Δxc = 0, and the jump in the slope of E(N) simply equals its orbital gap (here 0.17 for LSDA against the exact 0.472).

H₂⁺ at R = 10 bohr: which description does LSDA favor, and why is this an error?

Delocalized: σg −0.549 versus −0.473 on one atom, although the exact limit in this basis is −0.495. In exact theory both descriptions are degenerate in the limit, while LSDA artificially lowers the energy of fractional charges: 2·E(½) < E(1). The error is ≈ 140 kJ/mol.

Move the fraction of exact exchange a. How do the curvature of E(N) and the H₂⁺ error change?

The curvature and the delocalization error decrease: exact exchange bends E(N) upwards (concave), LSDA downwards (convex). At a = 0.25 (25 % exact exchange, as in PBE0; here it is mixed with LSDA, not with PBE) E(½) − ½E(1) = −0.037 instead of −0.050, and the error for H₂⁺ at R = 10 bohr is 118 kJ/mol. Even at a = 1 the self-interaction of LSDA correlation remains.
