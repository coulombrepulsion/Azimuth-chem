MO diagrams of diatomic molecules

Molecular orbitals are built as linear combinations of atomic orbitals (LCAO). Orbitals of the same symmetry and similar energy combine, and electrons fill the MOs according to the Pauli principle and Hund’s rule. The bond order and magnetic properties follow directly from the diagram. The energies come from a simple model (the extended Hückel method in a valence basis), so the diagram is qualitative.

What to understand

  • MOs are built from AOs of the same symmetry and similar energy: bonding MOs lie below the parent AOs, antibonding ones above.
  • The bond order bonding − antibonding2 correlates with bond length and strength.
  • The order of σ2p and π2p is set by s–p mixing: strong in B₂–N₂ and weak in O₂ and F₂.
  • Unpaired electrons in degenerate MOs explain the paramagnetism of O₂ and B₂.

O₂: how many unpaired electrons?

Unpaired electrons

2: twelve valence electrons, and the last two occupy the two degenerate π*2p orbitals one each with parallel spins (Hund’s rule). That is why O₂ is paramagnetic — liquid oxygen is attracted by a magnet. The valence-bond picture with a double bond O=O does not explain this.

What is the bond order of O₂⁻?

Bond order

1.5: (8 − 5) / 2. The added electron goes into an antibonding π* orbital, and the bond lengthens from 1.21 to 1.35 Å.

Select B₂ and switch off s–p mixing. What would the magnetic properties of B₂ be?

Diamagnetic: without mixing σ2p lies below π2p, and the last two electrons pair up in σ2p. Experiment shows that B₂ is paramagnetic (term ³Σg−) — direct evidence of s–p mixing.

How does the bond strength change on ionization O₂ → O₂⁺ (compare with N₂ → N₂⁺)?

O₂ loses an electron from an antibonding π* orbital: the bond order rises from 2 to 2.5, the bond shortens (1.208 → 1.116 Å) and strengthens (D₀ 5.12 → 6.66 eV). N₂ loses an electron from the bonding σ2p, and the bond weakens (order 3 → 2.5).

CO: on which atom is the HOMO (σ2p) mainly localized?

On carbon: about 70 % of the MO comes from the 2s and 2pz of the C atom. It is the lone pair of carbon, which is why CO binds to metals through C rather than O.

What is the bond order of C₂?

Bond order

2: eight valence electrons (σ2s)² (σ*2s)² (π2p)⁴. Both bonding pairs are π bonds and there is no σ bond “as such”; this is one of the consequences of s–p mixing.
