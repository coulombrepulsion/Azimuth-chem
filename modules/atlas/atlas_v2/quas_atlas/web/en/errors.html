The error of a calculation: from the error of a method to a conclusion

A number from a calculation without an error bar is not a result. Here — how the errors of the method, the basis and the convergence add up, how to go from the mean error on a test set to an interval for your own quantity, how isodesmic reactions and a related system cancel errors, and why conformer shares are so sensitive to them.

What to understand

  • Independent errors add in squares: σ = √(σ²_method + σ²_basis + σ²_convergence). The largest one decides the total, so the numerical error should be several times smaller than the error of the method — then it is invisible.
  • The error of a method is taken from test sets (GMTKN55, S66) — for your type of quantity. MAE = 0.80σ, σ = 1.25·MAE; 95 % of the errors lie within ±2σ: MAE 8 kJ/mol → ±20. A rate constant is then known to a factor e^{2σ/RT} ≈ 3000.
  • An isodesmic reaction has as many bonds of each type on the left as on the right, and systematic bond errors cancel. The enthalpy of formation of ethanol through C₂H₅OH + CH₄ → CH₃OH + C₂H₆: −232.9 kJ/mol against the experiment −234 ± 2.
  • A correction by a related system: ΔE_corr = ΔE_target + (ΔE_ref^exp − ΔE_ref^calc), σ² = 2σ²(1 − ρ). A similar reaction (ρ = 0.8) reduces the error from 8 to 5 kJ/mol, a dissimilar one (ρ = 0) raises it to 11.
  • The last link of the chain to the observable is an exponential. ΔG = 2 ± 4 kJ/mol between conformers gives the share of the second from 8 to 69 %: the calculation does not tell which one dominates. Another frequent correction is the standard state: 1 atm → 1 mol/L gives RT ln 24.5 = 7.9 kJ/mol per particle.

The error of the method is 8, of the basis 3, of the convergence 1 kJ/mol. The convergence was improved to 0.01. How does the total change?

√(64 + 9 + 1) = 8.6, √(64 + 9) = 8.5. The largest error decides the total.

The MAE of a functional for barriers is 8 kJ/mol. Within what interval is the error of your barrier with 95 % probability?

kJ/mol

σ = 1.25·MAE = 10, ±2σ = ±20. The mean error is not a bound: 42 % of the reactions err by more than MAE.

Why is the enthalpy of formation of ethanol more accurate through an isodesmic reaction than through atomization?

Through atomization the errors of all eight bonds add up (24 kJ/mol in the lecture's conventional numbers), in the isodesmic reaction they cancel.

A calculation gave ΔG = 2 ± 4 kJ/mol between two conformers. What is the honest statement?

The exponential turns ±4 kJ/mol into an interval of 8–69 %. A more accurate method for one difference narrows it: at σ = 1 — 23–40 %.
