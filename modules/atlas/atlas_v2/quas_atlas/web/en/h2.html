H₂ dissociation: RHF, UHF, and CI

A real calculation of H₂ in the minimal STO-3G basis (ζ = 1.24) at each internuclear distance R. Five ways to compute the same molecule, and the further the bond is stretched, the more they diverge. Choose R with the slider or by clicking on the plot.

What to understand

  • As H₂ is stretched, RHF keeps the ionic structure H⁺H⁻ on a par with the covalent one, and the energy rises too high.
  • CI mixes σg² and σu² and describes dissociation correctly — this is static correlation.
  • Near equilibrium correlation is dynamic, and MP2 recovers most of it; at large R MP2 fails: it is not variational.
  • UHF gets the correct limit by breaking spin symmetry.
  • The error of a calculation splits into three parts: unconverged SCF (addressed by bringing SCF to convergence), the method (compared with full CI in the same basis) and the basis (full CI against the exact energy). The decomposition is meaningful only under fixed conditions: the method error itself depends on the basis.
Model limits: full CI is the exact solution in the given basis

Covalent and ionic structures

Expand the wave functions in the atomic orbitals A and B. The covalent structure A(1)B(2) + B(1)A(2) has one electron on each atom (Heitler–London). The ionic structure A(1)A(2) + B(1)B(2) has both electrons on the same atom, H⁻H⁺ or H⁺H⁻. λ is the ratio of their coefficients.

Three kinds of error

At fixed R and basis the deviation from the exact energy splits into three parts, and each has its own remedy. An unconverged calculation: SCF was stopped before convergence, and it has to be converged (more iterations, and for poor convergence DIIS, damping or another initial guess; see the “SCF iterations” page). The limitation of the method: RHF or MP2 differ from full CI in the same basis, and a method that describes correlation better is needed. The limitation of the basis: even full CI (the exact solution in the given basis) differs from the exact energy, and a larger basis is needed. The parts are not independent: the method error itself depends on the basis, so the decomposition is meaningful only under the chosen conditions. The energies were computed in advance with the same Gaussian-basis HF, MP2 and CI program as on the Bader theory page; RHF starts from the core Hamiltonian, without DIIS acceleration. The exact energy at R = 1.4 bohr is −1.174475 Eh (Kołos and Wolniewicz), at R = 10 bohr it is the energy of two H atoms, −1 Eh.

Check your knowledge

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

Stretch H₂. What does it mean when the weight of the main configuration c₀ drops below 0.9?

One configuration is no longer enough: σu² is needed with a large weight — this is static correlation. It happens from about 3.2 bohr (≈ 1.7 Å), that is, at a moderately stretched bond.

Near equilibrium (R = 1.4 bohr): how much of the correlation energy does MP2 give, and why?

About two thirds (64% of −0.0206 a.u.): near equilibrium the correlation is dynamic, and perturbation theory handles it.

Turn on MP2. Why does the MP2 energy fall below the exact one at large R?

MP2 is not variational, and the denominator εσu − εσg tends to zero on stretching, so the correction grows without bound (below CI after about 6.6 bohr, below the exact limit −1 Eh after 7.6 bohr).

How does the RHF function differ from CI in the coefficient of the ionic structure H⁺H⁻?

In RHF the ionic and covalent structures always enter with equal coefficients (λ = 1) at any R. In CI, λ ≈ 0.29 at R = 1.4 bohr, and λ → 0 as the bond is stretched. λ is a ratio of coefficients, not a probability: at finite R the structures overlap.

Why does the UHF energy become close to the triplet energy at R = 6 bohr?

At large R both describe almost independent H atoms, and the singlet and triplet become almost degenerate. The UHF function is their mixture (⟨S²⟩ ≈ 1), so its energy is close to the common energy. The equality is only approximate: at 6 bohr UHF lies 0.5 kJ/mol above CI and 0.5 kJ/mol below the triplet, and at 4 bohr it differs from CI by about 21 kJ/mol.

Section “Three kinds of error”: R = 1.4 bohr, the 6-31G** basis, RHF, 1 iteration. What does the error consist of? What does each next step remove: “until convergence”, then full CI, then switching to STO-3G?

Error of the unconverged SCF, mEh

105.3 mEh in total: unconverged SCF 62.1, method 33.9 (the correlation energy in this basis), basis 9.3. Iterating to convergence removes 62.1 mEh, full CI another 33.9; the basis error remains. Switching to STO-3G increases it to 37.2 mEh, while the RHF method error in STO-3G is smaller, 20.6 mEh: the parts of the error depend on each other. Each part has its own remedy.

Now R = 10 bohr, RHF, converged. Will the method error decrease on going from 6-31G to 6-31G**?

No: about 248 mEh (650 kJ/mol) in both bases. A single RHF determinant cannot describe two separate H atoms; this is static correlation, and enlarging the basis does not remove it (in STO-3G the error is even larger, 337 mEh). A method that captures static correlation is needed, here full CI.
