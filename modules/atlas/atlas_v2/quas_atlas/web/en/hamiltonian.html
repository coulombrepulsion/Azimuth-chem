Molecular Hamiltonian

The full nonrelativistic Hamiltonian of a molecule consists of five types of terms: the kinetic energies of the nuclei and electrons, electron–nucleus attraction, electron repulsion and nuclear repulsion. Choose a molecule — the page writes the Hamiltonian in atomic units and counts the terms of each type.

What to understand

  • The molecular Hamiltonian contains the kinetic energy of electrons and nuclei and all Coulomb interactions.
  • The number of terms grows as the square of the number of particles; electron–electron repulsion is the main difficulty.
  • In the Born–Oppenheimer approximation the nuclei are fixed: TN is dropped, VNN becomes a number, and the electronic problem is solved.

CaH₂: how many terms are there in Vee?

Terms in Vee

N = 20 + 1 + 1 = 22 electrons; the number of pairs is N(N − 1)2 = 22·212 = 231.

Benzene: how many terms are there in VeN?

Terms in VeN

N = 6·6 + 6 = 42 electrons, M = 12 nuclei: every electron with every nucleus, N·M = 504.

Compare CH₄ and NH₄⁺. What is the same?

The numbers of nuclei (5) and electrons (10) are the same, so the numbers of terms of every type coincide too. Only the charges differ: in VeN and VNN of NH₄⁺ there is Z = 7 instead of 6.

Turn on “Nuclei fixed”. Why does VNN not enter the electronic Hamiltonian as an operator?

For given nuclear coordinates VNN is just a number. It shifts all electronic levels equally and does not change the wavefunctions, so it is added to Eel to give U(R).

How many times heavier than an electron is a proton?

How many times

1836 times. That is why nuclei move much more slowly than electrons — the Born–Oppenheimer approximation rests on this.

How does the number of terms in Vee change if the number of electrons doubles?

About 4 times: N(N − 1)2 ≈ N²2. Electron repulsion is the most numerous and the hardest part of the problem.
