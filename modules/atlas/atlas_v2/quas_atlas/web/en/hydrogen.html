The hydrogen atom and atomic orbitals

The hydrogen atom is the only atom for which the Schrödinger equation is solved exactly. The wavefunction separates into a radial part Rnl(r) and an angular part Ylm(θ, φ); the energy depends only on n: En = −Z²2n² hartree. Real orbitals (px, dxy, …) are shown here: these are the ones drawn in textbooks and used in quantum chemistry. They are obtained as linear combinations of the complex spherical harmonics Ylm: for example, px ∝ (Y1,−1 − Y1,1)/√2. The quantum number m is the projection of the orbital angular momentum on the z axis, and px, py have no definite m; m = 0 corresponds to pz.

What to understand

  • The state of the electron in the hydrogen atom is set by quantum numbers: n determines the energy, l the orbital angular momentum and shape, m the projection of the angular momentum on the chosen z axis. The real orbitals px, py are linear combinations of the states with m = +1 and m = −1, so they have no single value of m.
  • There are n − 1 nodes in total: n − l − 1 radial and l angular.
  • The energy depends only on n (degeneracy n²); in many-electron atoms the degeneracy in l is lifted.
  • Transitions between levels give a line spectrum: ν̃ ∝ 1n₁² − 1n₂².
Model limits: the exact solution for a hydrogen-like atom
What is computed
Orbitals, radial functions, energy levels and spectral series.
Which approximations are made
A non-relativistic Hamiltonian, a point nucleus; fine structure and spin are not included. Real orbitals are shown: combinations of the +m and −m states. Level energies and line wavelengths use the Rydberg constant of the given nucleus, with the reduced mass: for hydrogen the series limit is 13.598 eV (13.6057 eV for an infinitely heavy nucleus); wavelengths are vacuum wavelengths.
What can be concluded
The energies and nodes are exact in this model; they carry over to many-electron atoms only qualitatively.

How many radial nodes does the 4s orbital have?

Radial nodes

Three: n − l − 1 = 4 − 0 − 1 = 3. The plot of R(r) has three points where the function changes sign; in the cloud of dots they are empty spherical shells.

What is the mean radius ⟨r⟩ of hydrogen 1s (in a₀)?

⟨r⟩, a₀

⟨r⟩ = 3n² − l(l + 1)2Z = 1.5 a₀ ≈ 0.79 Å. It is larger than the most probable distance because the distribution has a long tail at large r.

Where is the maximum of the radial density r²R² for 1s (in a₀)?

r, a₀

At r = a₀ = 0.529 Å — the Bohr radius. The density |ψ|² itself is largest at the nucleus, but the volume of a thin spherical shell 4πr²dr goes to zero there.

Balmer series: the wavelength of the Hα line (transition 3 → 2), nm?

λ, nm

ν̃ = RH(12² − 13²) ≈ 15233 cm⁻¹, λ = 1ν̃ ≈ 656 nm — the red line of hydrogen. The other lines of the series: 486, 434, 410 nm.

What is the ionization energy of He⁺ from the ground state, eV?

E, eV

E = Z² · 13.6 eV = 4 · 13.6 ≈ 54.4 eV. Select the He⁺ nucleus: all levels drop by a factor of Z² = 4 and the orbitals shrink by a factor of 2.

Why do 2s and 2p have the same energy in the hydrogen atom?

In the pure Coulomb field of one nucleus the energy depends only on n (an “accidental” degeneracy). In many-electron atoms 2s penetrates closer to the nucleus than 2p, is less screened and lies lower — the degeneracy in l is lifted.
