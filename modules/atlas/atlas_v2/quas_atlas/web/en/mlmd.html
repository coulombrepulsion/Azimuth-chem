Molecular dynamics and machine-learning potentials

Molecular dynamics is hundreds of thousands of steps, and each needs the forces on all atoms. With DFT such a trajectory costs months, with a learned potential minutes. Here you see how to choose the time step, where a learned model can be trusted and what a trajectory is able to see at all.

What to understand

  • A machine-learning potential: the energy is a sum of atomic contributions, each depending on the surroundings within a cutoff sphere; the forces are exact derivatives. The cost grows linearly with the number of atoms, not as N³ like DFT.
  • A model interpolates: where there were no training structures it answers anything and will not say so itself. A committee of models (or a Gaussian process) gives a spread; where it is three times the usual, the structure is computed with DFT and the model retrained — active learning.
  • Velocity Verlet is reversible in time: the error of the energy does not pile up but oscillates and grows as Δt². The step is a tenth to a twentieth of the period of the fastest vibration: 0.5 fs with hydrogen, 1–2 fs for silicon.
  • The instantaneous temperature fluctuates by √(2/(3N)): ±10 % for 64 atoms. It is a property of a small system, not an error of the thermostat (Langevin, Nosé–Hoover).
  • Dynamics sees what happens in picoseconds to nanoseconds: vibrations, diffusion, melting. A reaction with a barrier of 80 kJ/mol — once in ten seconds — cannot be seen with any model: it needs a transition state and the Eyring theory.

Check your knowledge

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

An O–H bond, 3700 cm⁻¹: the period of the vibration is about 9 fs. Which time step to take?

Δt, fs

0.5 fs — about a twentieth of the period. At 2 fs the energy saws visibly, at 4–5 fs the integration blows up. For silicon without hydrogen (period 64 fs) 1–2 fs is enough.

The step of velocity Verlet was doubled. How does the oscillation of the total energy change?

Fourfold: the error ∝ Δt². But it does not pile up — the scheme is reversible in time. The energy grows without end with the Euler method.

64 atoms at 300 K. By how much does the instantaneous temperature fluctuate?

δT

±31 K: δT/T = √(2/(3·64)) = 0.10. It is a property of a small system: a sum of 192 squares of velocities. A cell of 6400 atoms would give ±3 K.

A model was trained on a crystal at 300 K, and the dynamics was run under pressure. The spread of the committee grew tens of times. What to do?

Compute with DFT and retrain: outside its training range the model invents forces, and the spread of the committee grows with the error. This is active learning: DFT computes only what the model does not know.
