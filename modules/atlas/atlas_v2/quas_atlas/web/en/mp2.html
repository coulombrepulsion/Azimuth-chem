Perturbation theory and MP2

The Møller–Plesset method splits the Hamiltonian into a “simple” part — the sum of Fock operators, for which the Hartree–Fock determinant is exact — and a perturbation, everything the Fock operator misses. The first correction for correlation, MP2 (second-order perturbation theory), recovers most of the correlation in N⁵ operations and is size-consistent. Here you can see where its formula comes from, when the perturbation series converges quickly and when poorly, and how much correlation MP2 gives for real molecules. The H₂ model in a minimal basis is solved right on the page, the rest was computed with PySCF.

What to understand

  • In the Møller–Plesset method the zeroth-order approximation is the sum of Fock operators; the first correction returns the Hartree–Fock energy, the second (MP2) is the first correction for correlation.
  • Each MP2 contribution is divided by a difference of orbital energies; at a small gap (stretched bonds, biradicals) MP2 overestimates correlation.
  • The Møller–Plesset series does not always converge well: with stretched bonds MP3 can be worse than MP2, the series oscillates, and in basis sets with diffuse functions it may even diverge.
  • MP2 is size-consistent and costs ~N⁵; for ordinary molecules it gives about 86–99 % of the CCSD(T) correlation energy in the same basis.
Model limits: small molecules, small basis sets

H₂ in a minimal basis: corrections order by order

The zeroth order is the sum of occupied orbital energies, 2εg. The first correction returns the Hartree–Fock energy. The second is the contribution of the single double excitation σg² → σu²: E(2) = −K² / 2(εu − εg). The numerator holds the same exchange integral that couples the configurations in CI, the denominator the difference of orbital energies.

How much correlation MP2 gives

The MP2 correlation energy compared with CCSD(T) for nine molecules in the cc-pVDZ basis (core electrons frozen, geometries close to experiment). CCSD(T) is the reference here: for these molecules it differs from full CI by 1–2 % of the correlation energy.

Why MP2 is size-consistent
The MP2 energy is a sum of contributions of individual electron pairs, without normalizing the whole function. For non-interacting molecules pairs from different molecules contribute nothing, and the energy adds up: for (H₂)ₙ in 6-31G the contribution per molecule is −17.397 mEₕ at n = 1, 2, 3 and 4.
Cost
The most expensive step of MP2 is transforming the two-electron integrals to the molecular-orbital basis, ~N⁵. That is an order of magnitude cheaper than CCSD (N⁶) and cheaper still than CCSD(T) (N⁷); with the RI approximation MP2 can be run for molecules of hundreds of atoms.
The small denominator
Each contribution is divided by a difference of orbital energies. If it is small (stretched bonds, biradicals, metals, aromatic systems with low-lying excitations), the contribution grows without bound — as for H₂ at large distance.
Where MP2 is used
Intermolecular interactions, conformational energies, geometries; in double-hybrid DFT functionals (for example B2PLYP) the MP2 contribution is added to the functional. Variants such as SCS-MP2 scale the same-spin and opposite-spin pair contributions separately.

H₂ in STO-3G, R = 0.75 Å: what share of the exact correlation energy does MP2 give, %?

MP2 share, %

About 64 %. In a minimal basis H₂ has one excitation σg² → σu², and its second-order contribution is noticeably smaller than the exact one: the rest comes in higher orders. In real basis sets the MP2 share for ordinary molecules is much higher (section 3).

Stretch the H₂ bond: at what R (to within 0.05 Å) does MP2 give exactly 100 % of the correlation, and more beyond it?

R, Å

About 3.5 Å. The gap εu − εg decreases, and it stands in the denominator of E⁽²⁾, so the correction grows faster than the exact correlation energy and then “overshoots”. Hitting 100 % here is a coincidence, not a sign of accuracy.

Water with doubled bond lengths: what is the error of MP3 relative to full CI, mEₕ?

MP3 error, mEₕ

About 64 mEₕ — more than for MP2 (45 mEₕ). Here the third order makes the result worse, and the series oscillates and converges slowly: “the higher the order, the better” is not guaranteed for perturbation theory.

Why is MP2, unlike CISD, size-consistent?

The MP2 energy is a sum of contributions of individual electron pairs, without normalizing the whole function. For non-interacting molecules pairs from different molecules contribute nothing, and the energy adds up: for (H₂)ₙ the contribution per molecule is the same for any n.

What share of the CCSD(T) correlation energy does MP2 give for methane in cc-pVDZ, %?

Share, %

About 86 % — the least among these molecules. The rest is higher-order contributions; so for reaction energies involving breaking C–H bonds MP2 errors can reach a few kcal/mol.
