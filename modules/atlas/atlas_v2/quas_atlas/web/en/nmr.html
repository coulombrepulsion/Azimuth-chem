NMR: from shielding to chemical shift

A nucleus with a magnetic moment precesses in a field B₀ at the Larmor frequency. The electrons around it weaken the field slightly — they shield it — so different atoms of one molecule resonate at slightly different frequencies. A program computes the shielding σ (the GIAO method), and the chemical shift δ is obtained by subtracting the σ of the reference — tetramethylsilane computed with the same method.

What to understand

  • The Larmor frequency ν₀ = γB₀/2π: for ¹H it is 42.58 MHz per tesla, a “400 MHz” spectrometer has a field of 9.4 T.
  • The shift δ = σ(TMS) − σ in parts per million (ppm) does not depend on the field; the reference must be computed with the same method and basis as the molecule.
  • n equivalent spin-½ neighbours give n + 1 lines with intensities from Pascal’s triangle; the splitting J in hertz does not depend on the field.
  • A good DFT calculation is accurate to 0.1–0.3 ppm for ¹H and 2–5 ppm for ¹³C; the solvent and the conformers change shifts by a comparable amount.
Model limits: an isolated molecule, a first-order spectrum
What is computed
Larmor frequencies of nuclei in a given field; shifts δ from computed shieldings σ and a reference (or a linear scale); first-order multiplets and the look of the spectrum.
Which approximations are made
First-order splitting (J much smaller than the frequency difference); all neighbours equivalent with one J; Lorentzian line shape; the shieldings are taken from your calculation as they are.
What can be concluded
How σ, δ, the field and J are related and how to read a computed spectrum — yes; second-order spectra, exchange, conformer averaging and solvent effects are not modelled here.

Your calculation: σ → δ and the spectrum

One line per group of equivalent nuclei: the shielding σ (ppm) from the output file (ORCA prints it in “CHEMICAL SHIELDING SUMMARY”, Gaussian as “Isotropic”), the number of nuclei, the number of equivalent neighbours and J (Hz). The reference is σ of the nucleus in TMS computed with the same method and basis.

The example is a teaching one: the σ of ethanol are chosen so that the shifts match the experimental ones in CDCl₃ (CH₃ 1.22, CH₂ 3.69, OH about 2.6 ppm; the OH shift depends on concentration and exchange). The δ axis of NMR grows from right to left.

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

Set the field to 14.1 T. At what frequency do protons resonate?

¹H frequency, MHz

600 MHz: ν = 42.577·14.1 = 600 MHz. Spectrometers are named by the proton frequency: a “600 MHz” one has 14.1 T.

The CH₃ multiplet of ethanol: the methyl group has the two CH₂ neighbours. What are the line intensities?

A 1:2:1 triplet: n = 2 neighbours give n + 1 = 3 lines with the coefficients of a row of Pascal’s triangle.

J = 7 Hz, n = 2, a 300 MHz spectrometer: how wide is the triplet in ppm?

Width, ppm

0.047 ppm: the width is 2J = 14 Hz, 14/300 = 0.047 ppm. At 600 MHz the same width takes half as much, 0.023 ppm, so multiplets do not overlap in a strong field.

“Your calculation”: σ(TMS) = 31.9 ppm, a proton has σ = 24.6 ppm. Which proton is it?

δ, ppm

δ = 31.9 − 24.6 = 7.3 ppm — the region of aromatic protons (benzene 7.3 ppm): the ring current of the π electrons deshields the protons outside the ring.
