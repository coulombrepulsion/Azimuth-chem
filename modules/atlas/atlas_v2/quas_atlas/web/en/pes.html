Potential energy surface

Reaction HA + HBHC → HAHB + HC, with the atoms on one straight line. In the Born–Oppenheimer approximation the energy depends only on two distances: r₁ = r(HA–HB) and r₂ = r(HB–HC). The surface is a model one (LEPS), close to the exact surface: barrier 0.40 eV, transition state at r₁ = r₂ = 0.93 Å. Click on the map to examine a point.

What to understand

  • PES minima are stable structures; first-order saddle points are transition states with one imaginary frequency.
  • A stationary point found must be checked by a frequency calculation: an optimization may stop at a saddle.
  • Zero-point vibrations change the effective barrier; the H–D difference gives the kinetic isotope effect.
  • If two states come close, the system can hop from one to the other (Landau–Zener model).
Model limits: a model LEPS surface, classical motion of the nuclei
What is computed
The collinear reaction H + H₂: the transition state, the reaction path, trajectories, the probability of hopping between terms.
Which approximations are made
The empirical LEPS surface: the barrier height is close to the exact H₃ surface, but the barrier itself is narrower (imaginary frequency at the saddle about 1980i cm⁻¹ versus roughly 1500i on the exact surface); the atoms are on a line; the nuclei move classically, without tunneling; hopping by the Landau–Zener formula.
What can be concluded
The qualitative patterns (barrier, isotope effect, the role of vibrations) are correct; rate constants cannot be obtained from it.

The horizontal axis is the path length s in atomic coordinates (Å); the path starts at the transition state (s = 0) and descends along the steepest slope in both directions: this is the intrinsic reaction coordinate (IRC). The familiar “hill” in reaction diagrams is exactly this cross-section, not the whole surface. The width of the valley at each point sets the frequency of the vibration across the path.

When one surface is not enough: avoided crossing

Example: the “harpoon” mechanism of Na + Cl. The covalent term Na + Cl is almost flat; the ionic term Na⁺ + Cl⁻ lies higher by IP(Na) − EA(Cl) = 1.53 eV but drops quickly because of the Coulomb attraction −14.4R. They cross at Rc ≈ 9.4 Å. The electronic coupling V turns the crossing into an avoided one: the adiabatic terms split by 2V.

Check your knowledge

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

Press “To transition state”. How does the imaginary frequency change when H is replaced by D?

It decreases by about a factor of √2: ≈ 1977i cm⁻¹ for H and ≈ 1398i cm⁻¹ for D — frequencies are inversely proportional to √m. These are frequencies of the LEPS surface; on the exact H₃ surface the barrier is wider, and the imaginary frequency is about 1500i cm⁻¹. The PES itself (barrier 0.40 eV at r₁ = r₂ = 0.93 Å) does not depend on the isotope.

Click at the point r₁ = r₂ ≈ 1.2 Å and start gradient descent. Where does it end up?

At the transition state, not a minimum: on the line r₁ = r₂ the gradient points along that line, and the descent goes straight into the saddle. That is why frequencies are always computed after an optimization.

A “collision” with H₂ zero-point vibrations (a series of phases): why does the reaction occur already below the barrier?

Part of the H₂ vibrational energy also goes into crossing the barrier: without vibrations the reaction starts at about 0.41 eV, while with zero-point vibrations at least one phase out of 24 reacts from about 0.2 eV. This is also classical zero-point leakage: in classical mechanics the H₂ vibrational energy can pass entirely into motion along the reaction path, which a quantum molecule cannot do.

Why is the effective barrier for D higher than for H once zero-point vibrations are included?

In the transition state the transverse vibration is softer than the H₂ vibration, and the zero-point energy “eats” part of the barrier (≈ 0.26 eV for H). For D all zero-point energies are smaller, the gain is smaller and the barrier higher (≈ 0.30 eV) — the basis of the kinetic isotope effect. This collinear model lacks the two bending vibrations of the transition state; with their zero-point energy (≈ 0.1 eV) the effective barrier is almost the classical one, ≈ 0.39 eV, and the isotope effect is smaller.

Avoided crossing: what happens as the coupling V between the states is reduced?

The hopping probability increases: the system more often stays on the diabatic state. At v = 1000 m/s, P = 0.5 at about V ≈ 0.011 eV; with stronger coupling the system follows the adiabatic state.
