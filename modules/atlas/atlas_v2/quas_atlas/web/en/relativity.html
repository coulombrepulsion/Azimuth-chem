Heavy elements: relativity and pseudopotentials

The Schrödinger equation knows nothing of relativity, and for light atoms this does not show. The inner electrons of a heavy atom, however, move at a speed comparable to that of light: their mass grows, the s shells contract, and chemistry changes — the colour of gold, liquid mercury, the lead–acid battery. A calculation takes this into account in two ways: a pseudopotential in place of the inner electrons or a relativistic Hamiltonian.

What to understand

  • The mean speed of the 1s electron of a hydrogen-like atom is Z atomic units, i.e. v/c = Z/137: 0.58 of the speed of light for gold.
  • The mass grows by γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²) and the s orbitals contract: the 1s of gold is 18 % smaller, the valence 6s contracts and is stabilised too.
  • A pseudopotential (ECP) replaces the inner electrons by a potential fitted to relativistic atomic calculations: in def2 these are 28 electrons for Rb–Xe, 46 for Cs–La and 60 for Hf–Rn.
  • Without an ECP relativity enters through the Hamiltonian (DKH2, ZORA, X2C) — with special all-electron basis sets, for example DKH-def2 or ZORA-def2 in ORCA.
Model limits: a hydrogen-like 1s electron, a point nucleus
What is computed
Speed, mass increase, radius contraction and the Dirac level of one 1s electron in the field of a nucleus Z; the core size of the def2 pseudopotentials.
Which approximations are made
One electron without screening by the others; a point nucleus; radius ∝ 1/m (the Bohr estimate); the valence shells of a many-electron atom are described only qualitatively.
What can be concluded
Why relativity matters for heavy elements and what a pseudopotential does — yes; the size of the effects on valence shells and chemical properties comes only from calculations with an ECP or a relativistic Hamiltonian.

Choose gold (Z = 79). At what fraction of the speed of light does the 1s electron move?

v/c

0.58: v/c = Z/137 = 79/137. The mass is then larger by γ = 1.22.

By how many per cent is the 1s radius of gold smaller than the non-relativistic one?

Contraction, %

By 18 %: √(1 − 0.58²) = 0.82. The valence 6s shell contracts too — it is closer to the nucleus and holds its electron more firmly, hence the nobility of gold.

Gold in a def2 basis: how many electrons are in the pseudopotential and how many are explicit?

60 in the core (1s–4f) and 19 explicit (5s, 5p, 5d, 6s): the Stuttgart–Cologne pseudopotential for Hf–Rn.

Bromine (Z = 35) in def2-TZVP — with a pseudopotential or without?

No ECP: in def2 the elements up to Kr are all-electron. Pseudopotentials start at rubidium (28 core electrons).
