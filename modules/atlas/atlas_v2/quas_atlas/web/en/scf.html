SCF iterations

A real Hartree–Fock–Roothaan calculation for a diatomic molecule in the STO-3G basis — one 1s function per atom, as in the classic treatment of H₂. Step through the self-consistency cycle and watch how the matrices, orbitals and energy change.

What to understand

  • The Fock operator depends on the orbitals themselves, so the Hartree–Fock–Roothaan equations FC = SCε are solved iteratively to self-consistency.
  • Convergence depends on the initial guess; DIIS speeds it up by extrapolating over the iteration history.
  • RHF describes bond breaking incorrectly; UHF gives the right limit at the cost of spin contamination.
  • Mulliken and Löwdin charges are a conventional partition of the density: they depend on the scheme and the basis.
  • By Koopmans’ theorem −ε of an occupied orbital estimates the vertical ionisation energy: orbital relaxation and correlation give corrections of opposite sign that partly cancel; −ε of a virtual one as an electron affinity is unreliable.
Model limits: Hartree–Fock in a minimal basis
What is computed
RHF and UHF iterations for H₂ and HeH⁺ in the STO-3G basis, energy curves, populations.
Which approximations are made
The HF method (no correlation); a minimal basis, one 1s function per atom.
What can be concluded
The mechanics of SCF are shown exactly; the converged energy is the solution of the HF equations in this basis, not the exact energy of the molecule.

Variational principle: the basis-function exponent ζ

  
The 1s basis function ~ e−ζr (here approximated by three STO-3G Gaussians). The larger ζ, the more “compact” the function. By the variational principle, the best function is the one that gives the lowest energy, and that is what we look for.

Population analysis: Mulliken and Löwdin

  
The number of electrons is N = ΣμνPμνSνμ = Tr(PS). The diagonal terms Pμμ are easily assigned to atoms, while the overlap population 2P12S12 belongs to the bond. Mulliken splits it equally between the atoms: NA = (PS)AA. Löwdin first symmetrically orthogonalizes the basis (χ′ = S−1/2χ), where there is no overlap: NA = (S1/2PS1/2)AA. The charge is qA = ZA − NA. The calculation is a converged RHF at the current R and ζ.

  

    

    

  





  
Orbital energies and Koopmans’ theorem

  
The ionisation energy in three ways in the same STO-3G basis: from the orbital energy (Koopmans), as the difference of the Hartree–Fock energies of the cation and the molecule (ΔSCF), and with the full CI of the neutral molecule. The cation has one electron, so its energy is exact in the basis.

HeH⁺ with default parameters: how many iterations are needed without DIIS and with DIIS? And at R = 3.5 bohr?






At R = 1.4632 bohr, 7 and 6; at R = 3.5 bohr, 13 and 7. When the bond is stretched, plain iterations converge more slowly, and DIIS gains more.


Step through the HeH⁺ iterations and follow the charge on H. How does it change, and why?






From +0.84 at the first iteration (F = h: no repulsion, the electrons sit almost entirely on He) to +0.53 after convergence: electron repulsion pushes part of the density toward hydrogen.


H₂ with the initial guess “both electrons on H(1)”. Where does the calculation converge?






To the symmetric solution with zero atomic charges, in 7 iterations. The calculation restores the molecular symmetry by itself.


The E(R) curve of H₂: what does ⟨S²⟩ ≈ 1 for UHF at R = 6 bohr mean?






The UHF function is a mixture of singlet (⟨S²⟩ = 0) and triplet (⟨S²⟩ = 2), i.e. spin contamination. UHF separates from RHF at about 2.2 bohr and describes the dissociation limit correctly at the cost of breaking spin symmetry; at finite R its energy is still above CI (by about 21 kJ/mol at 4 bohr).


Variational principle: how does the optimal ζ change when H₂ is compressed from 1.4 to 0.8 bohr?






It grows: ≈ 1.19 at 1.4 bohr and ≈ 1.37 at 0.8 bohr. On compression the functions “contract” toward the nuclei; in the limit R → 0 one gets a He atom with ζ ≈ 1.69.


Population analysis, HeH⁺ at R = 1.4632 bohr: which atom is more positive according to Mulliken, and which according to Löwdin?






By Mulliken, H (+0.530 versus +0.470 on He); by Löwdin, He (+0.527 versus +0.473 on H). The schemes disagree even on the sign of the difference: an atomic charge is not an observable but the result of an arbitrary partitioning.


Set ζ(H) = 1.6 in the section on the variational principle. What happened to the Mulliken charge of H, and what does it tell you?






It grew from +0.530 to +0.597 (Löwdin: +0.545), although the molecule is the same: Mulliken charges depend strongly on the basis.


H₂: why is the Mayer bond order equal to 1 at any R, even though the overlap population drops to zero?






RHF always puts both electrons into a single bonding MO, so it describes one two-electron bond even when the atoms are pulled apart. This is the same reason RHF describes the dissociation of H₂ incorrectly.


SCF has converged: the energy no longer changes in the tenth decimal place. Can one tell from this how close it is to the exact energy of the molecule?






It cannot be determined from these data. Convergence means only that the Hartree–Fock equations in this basis have been solved. The method error (no correlation) and the basis error remain: for H₂ at 1.4 bohr in the 6-31G** basis the converged RHF lies 43 mEh (about 113 kJ/mol) above the exact energy.


“Koopmans’ theorem”: HeH⁺ at R = 1.46 bohr. Which is larger — the Koopmans IP or the ΔSCF one?






The Koopmans IP is larger: −ε₁ = 43.5 eV, ΔSCF = 42.3 eV. The cation’s orbital rearranges and lowers its energy — this is relaxation; for H₂ in a minimal basis there is none, the orbital is fixed by symmetry.
