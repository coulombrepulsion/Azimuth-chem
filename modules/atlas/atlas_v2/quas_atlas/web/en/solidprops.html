Properties of crystals: compression, phonons, heat capacity

The total energy of a crystal at several volumes gives the equilibrium lattice constant and the bulk modulus; the second derivatives of the energy over the displacements of the atoms give the phonons; the phonons give the heat capacity and the thermochemistry. Here you see how each of these quantities is built and what spoils its calculation.

What to understand

  • The equation of state E(V): a series of calculations at several volumes is fitted by the Birch–Murnaghan formula, giving V₀, the bulk modulus B₀ = V·d²E/dV² and its pressure derivative B₀′.
  • B₀ needs an accurate energy: a range of volumes of ±5–10 %, a good convergence in k-points and Ecut. A noise of a few meV over a narrow range makes B₀′ meaningless.
  • Phonons are the vibrations of the lattice with a wave vector q. With two atoms per cell there is an acoustic branch (ω → 0 at Γ) and an optical one; the gap between them grows with the difference of the masses.
  • The heat capacity: at T ≪ θD Cv ∝ T³ (the Debye model), at T ≳ θD it is 3R per mole of atoms (the Dulong–Petit law). The Einstein model with one frequency gives too small a Cv at low T.
  • In Quantum ESPRESSO: E(V) — a series of pw.x and ev.x (or vc-relax); phonons — ph.x, q2r.x and matdyn.x; the phonon DOS gives the heat capacity and the entropy of the crystal.

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

Si, a range of ±1 %, 7 calculations, a noise of 5 meV. Which quantity does the fit still find sensibly?

V₀: the position of the minimum shows even through noisy points (19.92 Å³ against the reference 20.02). But the energy at the edge of the range changes by only ~0.6 meV — less than the noise, and B₀ comes out in thousands of GPa. To find B₀ you need a range of ±5–10 % or a smaller noise.

A chain with masses 40 and 10 amu, C = 50 N/m. How wide is the gap between the branches?

Gap, cm⁻¹

206 cm⁻¹: the top of the acoustic branch √(2C/mheavy) = 206 cm⁻¹, the bottom of the optical one √(2C/mlight) = 412 cm⁻¹ — twice as high, because the masses differ fourfold.

How do the atoms move in the optical branch at Γ?

Against each other: the centre of mass of the cell stays in place. The shift as a whole is the acoustic branch at Γ (ω = 0). Only the heavy atoms vibrate in the acoustic branch at the zone boundary.

Diamond (θD = 2230 K) at 300 K. What is Cv/3R roughly?

Cv/3R

≈ 0.17: room temperature is far below θD, and most vibrations of diamond are «frozen». Copper (θD = 343 K) at the same temperature nearly reaches the Dulong–Petit limit: 0.94.
