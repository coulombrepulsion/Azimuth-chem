Spin states of complexes in DFT

High or low spin in an iron(II) complex is a question different functionals answer differently: the energy difference depends almost linearly on the share of exact exchange. Here — where this dependence comes from, how to check a solution by ⟨S²⟩ and why each spin state is optimized separately.

What to understand

  • Exchange gives −K for every pair of electrons with parallel spins. d⁶: high spin (5α + 1β) — 10 pairs, low (3α + 3β) — 6. Hartree–Fock (exact exchange, no correlation) puts the high spin too low, pure GGAs (self-interaction, excess delocalization) the low one.
  • A hybrid with the share of exact exchange a: ΔE(a) ≈ ΔE(0) − k·a. For Fe(II): k = 1.5 (water), 4.7 (NCH), 7.8 (CO) kJ/mol per 1 % — the larger the more covalent the bond. For [Fe(NCH)₆]²⁺ the sign changes near 19 %: BP86 — low spin, B3LYP — high.
  • The rule: 10–15 % of exact exchange (TPSSh, B3LYP*), a conclusion only if it holds for two or three functionals; calibration on a related complex of known spin; a reference NEVPT2 or CCSD(T) for a small model.
  • Spin contamination: ⟨S²⟩ of the exact function is S(S + 1) (singlet 0, triplet 2, quintet 6), for UKS ⟨S²⟩ = S(S + 1) + N_β − Σ|⟨φ^α|φ^β⟩|² ≥ S(S + 1). The admixture of the state S + 1 is the excess divided by the distance to it: 6.9 for a quintet — 15 % of the septet. More checks: the spin on the metal (≈ 3.6–3.9 for HS Fe(II)) and the d-orbital populations.
  • The high spin fills the antibonding e_g*: the Fe–L bonds are 0.1–0.4 Å longer (the DFT error in a length is 0.02 Å). Each state is optimized with its own multiplicity and the energies are compared at the minima — the adiabatic difference.

Check your knowledge

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

How many pairs of electrons with parallel spins are in the d shell of high-spin d⁶ (5α + 1β)?

10 = C(5,2): all the pairs among five α electrons. Low spin (3α + 3β) has 3 + 3 = 6. The four extra pairs are why exact exchange favours the high spin.

For [Fe(NCH)₆]²⁺ BP86 gives +100 kJ/mol (low spin), B3LYP −17 (high). What to do?

The sign depends on the share of exchange (it changes near 19 %), so the question is open. Two or three functionals with different shares, calibration on experiment and, if possible, a NEVPT2 or CCSD(T) reference.

A quintet (S = 2), the output says ⟨S²⟩ = 6.9. What is the admixture of the septet?

(6.9 − 6)/(12 − 6) = 0.15. Such a solution cannot be trusted; high-spin Fe(II) usually gives 6.00–6.05.

Why are the high-spin and the low-spin states optimized separately?

In the high spin two electrons sit on the antibonding e_g*, and the bonds are much longer. The energy difference at the own minima is the adiabatic one.
