Atomic terms

One electron configuration, such as 2p², corresponds to several states with different energies — the terms 2S+1L. They are found from microstates: list all ways of placing the electrons in spin orbitals allowed by the Pauli principle and group them by ML and MS. Hund’s rules give the ground term.

What to understand

  • One configuration gives several terms 2S+1L with different energies.
  • Terms are found from the microstate table: each term takes (2L + 1)(2S + 1) states.
  • For equivalent electrons the Pauli principle forbids some of the terms.
  • The ground term follows from the Hund rules: maximum S, then maximum L, then J according to shell filling.
Model limits: LS coupling and the Hund rules
What is computed
Microstates of a configuration, the decomposition into terms, the ground term.
Which approximations are made
The Russell–Saunders scheme (LS coupling); the spin–orbit splitting into J levels is not computed. For configurations with several open shells (e.g. 3d²4p) Hund's rules for the ground term are unreliable.
What can be concluded
The set of terms is exact in the LS scheme; the Hund rules predict the ground term but not the order of the excited ones.

2. Ground term from Hund’s rules

1) Maximum multiplicity: electrons occupy different orbitals with parallel spins. 2) Then maximum L: fill ml starting from the largest. 3) J = |L − S| if the subshell is less than half full, and J = L + S if it is more than half full.

How many microstates does the p² configuration have?

Microstates

6!2! · 4! = 15: the two electrons are distributed over six spin orbitals without repetition (Pauli principle).

Which terms does the p² configuration give?

¹D, ³P and ¹S: 5 + 9 + 1 = 15 microstates. The terms ¹P, ³D and ³S are absent — for equivalent electrons they are forbidden by the Pauli principle (compare with 2p¹3p¹ in section 1).

What is J in the ground term of the carbon atom?

J

J = 0: term ³P₀. The 2p² subshell is less than half full, so J = |L − S| = |1 − 1| = 0.

How many microstates does the d² configuration have?

Microstates

10!2! · 8! = 45. They split into the terms ¹G, ³F, ¹D, ³P, ¹S; the ground term is ³F (as for Ti²⁺ and V³⁺).

What is J in the ground term of the iron atom?

J

J = 4: term ⁵D₄. 3d⁶: five electrons spin up and one down, S = 2, L = 2; the subshell is more than half full, so J = L + S = 4.

Why is the ground term of oxygen ³P₂, but that of carbon ³P₀?

The 2p⁴ subshell of O is more than half full, and by Hund’s third rule J = L + S = 2. For C (2p²) it is less than half full, J = |L − S| = 0. The terms of p² and p⁴ are the same; only the order of the J levels differs.
