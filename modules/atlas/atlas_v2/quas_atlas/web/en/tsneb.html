Searching for a transition state and the reaction path

A minimizer finds a minimum by itself: it rolls down and stops. A transition state is a pass between pits, and one cannot roll down to it. Here, on model surfaces, you see how the saddle is reached by eigenvector following, how an elastic band finds the reaction path, why a scan can mislead and how to prove that the right saddle was found.

What to understand

  • A transition state is a first-order saddle point: zero gradient, exactly one negative eigenvalue of the Hessian (one imaginary frequency). BFGS keeps its Hessian positive and rolls down from near a saddle into a minimum.
  • Eigenvector following: in the basis of the modes the step goes up along the reaction coordinate and down along the others; the step is limited by a trust radius. A good start is needed: the region of convergence to a saddle is narrower than the bowl of a minimum.
  • The nudged elastic band (NEB): a chain of images between the reactant and the product; of the true force the part across the path is taken, of the springs the part along it. The climbing image (CI-NEB) has no spring, climbs up along the path and converges to the saddle.
  • A relaxed scan needs a chosen coordinate. If the reaction runs along two coordinates at once, the profile breaks, and the scans forward and backward diverge. The maximum of a scan is a start, not a transition state.
  • Three checks of the point found: one imaginary frequency; its mode is the motion of the reaction; the IRC from the saddle reaches the reactant and the product.

Check your knowledge

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

E = (x² − 1)² + y². What are the eigenvalues of the Hessian at the saddle (0, 0)?

−4 and 2: the Hessian is diag(12x² − 4, 2), at x = 0 it is (−4, 2). One negative — a first-order saddle; two negative would be a summit.

Why does BFGS started near a saddle not find the transition state?

BFGS «believes» it is in a pit and goes down — into the reactant or the product. A saddle search needs a Hessian with one negative value and the knowledge of which mode is the reaction coordinate.

A band was optimized without projections: the true force and the springs added whole. What barrier came out?

Too low: the springs across the path pull the images off the valley floor, and the true force along the path drags them to the ends — no one is at the top. On the page 68 instead of 106.

What proves that the right transition state was found?

All three checks: a zero gradient is found at a minimum and at a higher-order saddle too; the maximum of a scan was not checked by the Hessian. Only the IRC ties the saddle to the right reactant and product.
