Atomic units

In atomic units the electron mass, its charge, Planck’s constant ħ and 4πε₀ are equal to one. The equations lose their constants, and typical quantities in atoms and molecules become numbers of order one. Quantum chemistry programs compute in atomic units, and their output is usually in them too.

What to understand

  • In atomic units ħ = me = e = 4πε₀ = 1, and the equations of quantum chemistry are written without constants.
  • The unit of length is the bohr (0.529 Å), of energy the hartree (27.2 eV ≈ 2625 kJ/mol).
  • Chemical energies are small fractions of a hartree, while total molecular energies are tens to hundreds of hartree.
  • Unit conversion is a routine step when reading program output files.

What is computed
Conversion of quantities between atomic units and SI, energy scales in chemistry.
Which approximations are made
There are none: atomic units are just a choice of scale (ħ, me, e and 4πε₀ are taken as 1), not a model. The conversions to SI use the CODATA 2018 recommended values of the constants; an error is possible only in their last digits.
What can be concluded
The conversions are exact; the “typical” energies on the scale are orders of magnitude.

Where the bohr and the hartree come from

An estimate of the size of the hydrogen atom from the uncertainty relation: if the electron is confined to a region of radius r, its momentum is p ~ ħr and its kinetic energy ~ ħ²2mer². Compression gains in the attraction −e²4πε₀r but loses in kinetic energy. The sum has its minimum at r = a₀ = 4πε₀ħ²mee²: this is the bohr, and the energy at the minimum is −½ hartree.
