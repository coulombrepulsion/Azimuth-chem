Walsh diagrams: molecular shape from orbitals

Why is water bent while BeH₂ is linear, although both have two A–H bonds? Walsh proposed an answer that needs no total-energy calculation: follow how the energy of each valence orbital changes on bending and add up the energies of the occupied ones. The shape is set by the number of valence electrons — and by the orbitals they go into.

What to understand

  • On bending AH₂ the symmetry drops from D∞h to C₂ᵥ: σg → 1a₁, σᵤ → 1b₂, and the πᵤ pair splits into 2a₁ and 1b₁.
  • 2a₁ drops strongly on bending — the s orbital of the central atom mixes in; 1b₁ (p perpendicular to the plane) does not depend on the angle, 1b₂ rises slightly.
  • Four valence electrons (BeH₂) fill only 1a₁ and 1b₂ — the molecule is linear; from five (BH₂) on, 2a₁ is occupied and the molecule is bent.
  • Walsh’s rule is qualitative: the sum of orbital energies is not the total energy, but it gets the trend along BeH₂ → H₂O right.
Model limits: a one-electron model, rigid bonds
What is computed
The energies of the six valence orbitals of AH₂ at angles from 80° to 180°, the sum of the occupied orbital energies and the angle of its minimum for 4–8 valence electrons.
Which approximations are made
A Hückel-type Hamiltonian: α for s and p of the central atom and for H 1s, β proportional to the direction cosines; fixed bond length; no electron repulsion; qualitative parameters, the same for all AH₂.
What can be concluded
Why the shape depends on the number of electrons and which orbitals set it — yes; exact angles and inversion barriers come only from total-energy calculations (HF, DFT).

Exercises on the page: do the action on the page and choose an answer — it is checked right away.

Choose BeH₂. Why is the molecule linear?

Four valence electrons fill 1a₁ and 1b₂. 1b₂ (from σᵤ) is destabilised on bending, and 2a₁, which would gain from bending, is empty.

Which orbital drops the most on bending AH₂?

2a₁: in the linear molecule it is one of the πᵤ, without overlap with the H atoms; on bending it starts to bond with H and the s of the central atom mixes in.

Why are the angles of NH₂ and H₂O almost the same (103° and 104.5°)?

The seventh and eighth valence electrons go into 1b₁ — the p orbital perpendicular to the molecular plane: it is non-bonding and does not depend on the angle, so the angle hardly changes.

How many valence electrons does AH₂ need for the model to give a bent molecule?

Valence electrons

From five on (BH₂, 131°): the fifth electron goes into 2a₁, which is stabilised on bending. With 4 electrons (BeH₂) the molecule is linear.
